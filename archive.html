IFT6162 · Mohamed Samir · Final course report

Better forecasts.
Better decisions?

A solar farm and a battery share one grid connection. Their controller must choose when to sell energy, store it, or hold capacity for reserve.

We test learned forecasts inside rolling-horizon optimization on real Australian market data. The winner changes with the period. Prediction error alone does not tell the whole story.

One error score cannot rank decisions.

In December, D lowers price MSE from 1,194.45 to 900.02 versus A, yet earns AUD 1,034.90 less under enabled/stress. Both use point MPC and the same solar forecaster. In March, B improves forecast scores and value together.

What differs between the controllers?
Rule
Training-price quantile rule (0.4 / 0.6), with idle reserve.
A · Point MPC
Ridge price forecast (α = 100), deterministic rolling-horizon MILP.
B · Scenario MPC
Eight joint energy/reserve/solar scenarios, ensemble copula coupling, CVaR α = 0.75 and risk weight 0.25. Fully matured residuals update causally.
D · Value-selected ridge
Price ridge α = 10,000, selected using earlier September validation value; same point planner and solar forecaster as A.
C · SPO+ selection
The predeclared three-seed selector chose initialization. All seeds equal A here, with zero new gradient updates. C is an alias, not an independent success for trained decision-focused learning.

All planners replan every five minutes with a six-hour horizon. A/B compares complete pipelines, including centering and risk; it does not isolate probabilistic information alone.

03 / Dispatch lab

Follow the battery.

Inspect an actual saved controller trajectory. The controls select recorded runs; they do not retrain a model or recompute a policy.

On a narrow screen, scroll charts horizontally. Times are interval ends in NEM time (fixed UTC+10). Each operating day runs 00:05 through next-day 00:00. The cursor reports exact exported values; charts use the same recorded five-minute rows.

Activation and accounting assumptions

Synthetic stress calls the full enabled reserve for 300 seconds at interval-ending 12:00, 12:05 and 12:10, plus 57 full-power-equivalent seconds at 18:00. This is a constructed stress pattern, not historical activation or an estimated contingency rate.

Activation energy enters export and energy cash once, reduces stored energy through discharge efficiency, and incurs wear. Capacity payment is reserve price × enabled MW × 1/12 hour. The 300-second energy guard is a conservative model envelope, not a claim about mandatory R6 sustain duration.

Operating value = energy cash + reserve capacity cash − modeled wear + terminal inventory change valued at a common training-median price within each block. Capex, network loss factors, recovery charges, PFR/FPP and detailed aging are excluded.

Not a universal winner.

Primal–dual certificates checked. Rule trajectories do not call an optimizer.

New extreme spikes

No interval reaches AUD 1,000/MWh in these locked blocks. Earlier October/November spike regressions stay separate.

Chronology

Each block uses 14 days for fitting and 7 for calibration before context and testing. Previously selected settings remain fixed. Some raw monthly archives were already available; this is a local predeclared controller evaluation, not public preregistration or pristine data acquisition.

What remains open

More regions and seasons, stronger planning baselines, activation uncertainty and realistic market clearing are needed before broad claims. These results establish neither commercial profit, emissions savings nor a general advantage for trained SPO+ or reinforcement learning.

Final course report (V49.3.2). The price-contingent reserve study closes with a boundary-layer result and a set of negative findings: V46 beats the scalar R = 16 by +147.218 AUD/event (14/14), but a tuned constant R = 15.80 captures 90.1% of the total gain; the effective intervention is a 0.4392 MWh layer; local reserve shadow does not rank long-term advantage (Spearman −0.098); and the joint inventory family has no representable case in 63 tested configurations, with 49 converged certificates and 14 unconverged. Read the 16-page report or the 15-slide deck. The result is a frontier and identifiability statement, not an adaptive-controller superiority claim.
